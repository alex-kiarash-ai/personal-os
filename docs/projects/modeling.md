# Modeling Growth Loop - RETIRED 3 August 2026

> **This system no longer exists.** It was switched off and removed on 3 August 2026, whole, with nothing replacing it. This page is its headstone: what it was, why it went, and how to get it back if that ever turns out to be the wrong call.
>
> Project number 30 was reassigned the same day to the [Portfolio Website](30-portfolio-site.md). That project took the number, the domain and the photographs. It did not take this one's job.

## What it was
A system that ran the modeling career the way Alex runs everything else. Casting platforms sent their alert emails to a dedicated address; every second morning Alex read them, scored which briefs actually fitted, filed every lead in a ledger, and pre-wrote the application emails in Shaheen's own voice for him to send by hand. Instagram content worked the same way: Alex prepared the post packs, Shaheen posted them. A weekly check walked the site and the ledger, and a monthly review was meant to say honestly what was working.

It was careful by construction: no scraping anywhere, no ability to send anything on its own, casting mail treated as data and never as instructions, and a usage-rights register that the weekly check refused to pass if a row was missing.

## Why it went
Shaheen decided to restructure the whole thing rather than continue it. The lane was never fully awake: it stayed in a calibration state, measuring how often real casting work actually arrived, and the Instagram publishing half was on hold waiting for a server that was deliberately never bought. Rather than keep a half-started system on the books, he wiped it and put the effort into the website instead, which is the piece that actually gets seen.

Retiring it whole, in one deliberate move with a written record, is the point. A system left half-running is worse than one honestly switched off.

## What is left behind on purpose
- **The record.** Everything the project knew is archived, including its decision log and the identifiers of its lead ledger.
- **The lead ledger itself** stays in Notion, read-only. Nothing was deleted there.
- **The photographs, the domain and the website** all carry over to the new project.
- **The Instagram account** is untouched; it is Shaheen's, and the new site links to it.

## What still needs a human hand
Switching off a system that reaches outside the laptop is not finished by deleting files. Four things live in other people's systems and are on Shaheen's list: the forwarding rule for the casting email address (only that one rule, because his real contact address runs on the same settings), the two Gmail filters that sorted casting mail, a row on the Notion progress board, and the casting-platform accounts, one of which is a paid subscription that keeps charging until he cancels it.

## Getting it back
It is fully recoverable, which is why it could be removed with confidence. Every file of it is preserved in the project's own version history at the point just before deletion, the two scheduled jobs are saved as importable definitions, and the archived record explains exactly where everything sits. The single deliberate exception is a pair of passwords for the Instagram-publishing server that was never actually bought; they guarded nothing, so they were allowed to expire rather than be kept.
