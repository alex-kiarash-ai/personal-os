## What the numbers say, before you tag anything

**The lane split is lopsided.** Fitness 11 · Commercial 10 · Hair 3.

**And "Commercial" is thinner than 10 suggests: eight of those ten frames are the same tennis campaign** (#3, 5, 8, 10, 14, 18, 20, 22). Only two are genuinely different commercial looks: the clean white-tee (#12) and the tailoring shot (#16). So the honest read of the grid is roughly: one big sportswear campaign, a strong fitness body of work, two tailoring frames, and three hair frames.

Three consequences worth deciding on rather than discovering at launch:

1. **A casting director scrolling this sees a fitness/sportswear model.** If that is the positioning you want, the grid is already right and just needs trimming. If you want commercial and tailoring booked too, the A-tier needs to over-weight #12 and #16 and the new site needs more of that look than exists today.
2. **Hair is under-served at 3 frames** while it is one of the three intent pages the old site ran for search. Either it earns more frames or it stops being a headline lane.
3. **Repeating one campaign eight times reads as a thin book**, even when each frame is good. A tighter grid of ~16-20 with fewer near-duplicates will look stronger than 24 with eight tennis frames.

**Variety check (PROJECT.md's own requirement):** close-up, half body and full body are all present. **The one strong horizontal for the hero still needs confirming** - the grid is uniformly 4:5 portrait and `hero.jpg` is the only candidate. Worth checking that it holds up wide on a desktop screen before the design phase locks.

**Three frames carry a co-model** (#5, 18, 22). Allowed under the solo rule as supporting frames, and you wanted them in for production value. Two notes: they must not lead a page, and the rights column matters more for them, because the co-model consented to the campaign, not necessarily to a public code repository.

## Alt text needs rewriting anyway

The existing alt text is split between two styles: the older frames use em-dashes, the newer ones use commas. Em-dashes break the voice rule, and alt text is user-facing copy. It gets rewritten during the build, so no action from you here - just don't treat the current alts as final.

## The rights column, and why it is not busywork

The whole set was shot professionally. Displaying them on a website is a grant you already have. **Putting the web-sized files in a public GitHub repository is a different grant** - cloneable, forkable and permanent in history. That is what the photographer ask covers (`portfolio-rights-ask`). Mark anything you are unsure about, especially the three co-model frames. If the answer comes back narrowed, nothing is wasted: the fallback keeps the repo public and pulls masters from private storage at build time.

## Reference sites - pick 3 (PROJECT.md open item 3)

**These five, and only these five, were actually opened and verified live during the 2026-07-18 site review.** They are reused here rather than re-researched, so nothing on this list is a guess. Re-open them when you pick; they may have changed since July.

| Site | Why it is on the list | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **orancusack.com** | Judged the strongest direct comparable in the review: polaroids gallery, season-stamped campaigns, an agency page, a booking path that actually converts | Heavier than a 4-page v1; don't copy the whole structure |

| **pietroboselli.com** | Celebrity-scale presentation, useful for how a physique-led book is sequenced | **No visible booking contact.** The review's sharpest finding: independents win on contact friction, so do the opposite here |
| **oliviabannerman.com** | The Digitals-tab convention done properly, for when `/digitals` comes back | Different market; borrow the pattern, not the styling |

Five is enough to pick three from, and every row is grounded. **If you want a wider field, say so and Alex will run a fresh verified sweep** rather than padding this table with names nobody has opened.

**How to pick:** one line each on what to steal and what to avoid. What matters is the *decision path* - how fast a booker gets from landing, to seeing your best frame, to knowing how to contact you. Pick three that differ from each other, not three variations of the same look.
